Done in conjunction with **CMU Students Using Data for Good**, on the Allegheny Land Trust's (ALT) St. John's project, with Audrey Yang and Jenny Lyu.

## The question

Has community participation in citizen science grown faster around the ALT's St. John's project (in Pittsburgh's Brighton Heights neighborhood) than we would expect from general trends?

## Approach

- Pulled iNaturalist observations for St. John's through the iNat API, since aggregate iNat data isn't available for a like-for-like comparison.
- Used eBird as the baseline: checklist counts for Pennsylvania and for Allegheny County, plus eBird activity in Brighton Heights itself.
- Compared annual growth rates from 2021 to 2025, using two different data sources so that the limits of each (eBird is less localized; iNat can't be compared to a same-source baseline) partly offset each other.

## Findings

| Series (2021–2025) | Annual growth |
|---|---|
| iNat observations, St. John's | 35.7% |
| eBird observations, Brighton Heights | 27.8% |
| eBird checklists, Allegheny County | 12.6% |
| eBird checklists, Pennsylvania | 7.4% |

St. John's iNat growth was about 4.8× the Pennsylvania baseline and about 2.8× the Allegheny County baseline. Brighton Heights eBird growth was about 3.7× and 2.2× those baselines. The write-up concludes that the pattern is consistent with the ALT project boosting local engagement, while noting that correlation doesn't imply causation.
